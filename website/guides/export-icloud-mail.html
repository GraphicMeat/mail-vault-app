How to Export iCloud Mail to Your Computer

6 min read

Apple doesn't provide a one-click "export my mail" button for iCloud Mail, which surprises a lot of people looking to back up an @icloud.com, @me.com, or @mac.com account. The good news is iCloud Mail fully supports standard IMAP access, which means any IMAP-capable mail client or backup tool can download your mail — you just need to set it up correctly, including one Apple-specific step: an app-specific password.

Step 1: Generate an app-specific password

Apple requires two-factor authentication on your Apple ID, and doesn't allow third-party apps to sign in with your normal Apple ID password. Instead, you generate a separate app-specific password just for mail access:

  • Go to appleid.apple.com and sign in.
  • Under Sign-In and Security, choose App-Specific Passwords.
  • Click Generate an App-Specific Password, give it a label like "Mail backup," and Apple shows you a one-time password.
  • Use that password (not your regular Apple ID password) when your mail client or backup tool asks you to sign in.

Step 2: iCloud Mail's IMAP settings

  • IMAP server: imap.mail.me.com, port 993, SSL/TLS.
  • SMTP server: smtp.mail.me.com, port 587, SSL/TLS, authentication required.
  • Username: your full iCloud email address (including @icloud.com, @me.com, or @mac.com).

Step 3: Connect a mail client or archiving tool and download everything

With the app-specific password and IMAP settings above, any standard mail client can connect and sync your iCloud mail. If you're just backing up (not planning to use the client day-to-day), a dedicated archiving tool is a better fit than a full mail client, since it's built to download and preserve everything rather than just display your inbox.

Why export iCloud Mail at all

A few common reasons people back up iCloud Mail specifically: iCloud storage tiers are notoriously small on the free plan (5 GB shared across Mail, Photos, and device backups), so mail often gets caught in the squeeze; some people want a copy of their email that doesn't depend on staying inside the Apple ecosystem; and some are moving away from Apple hardware entirely and want their mail history to come with them.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do I need an app-specific password for iCloud Mail?

Apple requires two-factor authentication on Apple IDs and doesn't let third-party mail clients sign in with your regular Apple ID password. Generate a separate app-specific password at appleid.apple.com under Sign-In and Security, and use that instead.

What are iCloud Mail's IMAP server settings?

IMAP server imap.mail.me.com on port 993 with SSL/TLS. For sending mail, SMTP server smtp.mail.me.com on port 587 with SSL/TLS and authentication required. Use your full iCloud address as the username.

Does iCloud have a built-in mail export feature like Google Takeout?

No. Apple doesn't offer a dedicated one-click export for iCloud Mail. The practical way to get your mail out is connecting a mail client or backup tool over IMAP with an app-specific password.

Can I export iCloud Mail on Windows?

Yes, in principle, with any Windows mail client that supports IMAP and an app-specific password. MailVault itself currently supports macOS and Linux, not Windows.
