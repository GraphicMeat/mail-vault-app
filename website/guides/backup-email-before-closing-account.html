How to Back Up Your Email Before Closing an Account

7 min read

Closing an email account is one of the few truly irreversible actions on the internet. Once a provider deletes an account — whether you close it yourself, a company shuts down a work email after you leave, or a domain lapses and takes its mailboxes with it — the emails are usually gone permanently, sometimes within days. There's no undo button, and support tickets asking to restore a closed account almost never work. If you're even considering closing an account, back it up first. Here's how to do it properly.

Step 1: Confirm the account still has full access

Do this before any deprovisioning starts. If it's a work account tied to your last day, back up before your final day, not after — IT often revokes access at close of business, sometimes before. If it's a personal account you're closing voluntarily, back up before you initiate the closure, since some providers begin wiping data immediately once you confirm.

Step 2: Download every folder, not just the inbox

People remember to back up their Inbox and forget Sent, Drafts, Archive, and any custom folders or labels. Sent mail is often the most valuable part of a backup — it's the only record of what you actually said, promised, or agreed to. Go through every folder deliberately.

Step 3: Choose a backup format you can actually use later

Most providers offer some kind of built-in export (Google Takeout, Outlook's .pst export, etc.), producing one large archive file. That's fine as a raw insurance copy, but it's painful to search or browse without importing it somewhere first. The more practical approach is an IMAP-based backup that saves each email as its own file — readable individually, searchable with normal file search, and not locked into one provider's export format.

Step 4: Verify before you close anything

This is the step people skip and regret. Before you click "delete account" or hand back a company laptop, actually open several archived emails, confirm attachments open correctly, and search for a specific message you remember receiving. A backup you haven't verified is a backup you're only assuming works.

Step 5: Store the backup somewhere outside the account you're closing

Obvious, but worth stating: your backup needs to live on a device or storage you control, not in a Drive or cloud storage tied to the same account you're about to delete. A local folder on your computer, backed up to an external drive or your own cloud storage of choice, is the safest bet.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I recover emails after an email account has been permanently deleted?

Almost never. Most providers permanently purge account data within days to weeks of closure, and support requests to restore a closed account are rarely honored, especially for free consumer accounts. Back up before closing, not after.

Do I need to back up Sent and Drafts, or just the Inbox?

Back up every folder. Sent mail is often the only record of what you actually wrote, and Drafts can contain unfinished but important messages. An Inbox-only backup misses a large part of your real email history.

What's the safest place to store an email backup?

A local folder on a device you control, ideally also copied to an external drive or a cloud storage account separate from the one you're closing. Never rely on storage tied to the account you're about to delete.

How do I know my backup actually worked before I close the account?

Open several archived emails and confirm they display correctly, check that attachments open, and search for a specific message you remember to confirm it's present. Do this before you initiate account closure, not after.
