A comfortable everyday starting point

Apple’s Mail guide covers managing multiple accounts, blocking senders, and automatically sorting messages into categories. It also documents Writing Tools with Apple Intelligence on supported setups. Availability depends on the system, language, and region, so a Mac running Mail does not automatically mean every AI feature is available. Official guidance ↗

Imagine a personal inbox with booking confirmations, school messages, and the occasional long family thread. A built-in client reduces setup decisions. Try it before buying something else: account setup, finding an attachment, and replying from the correct address are sensible first checks. If all three work comfortably, the next choice is about the life of the messages, not the attractiveness of the inbox.

An exported mailbox is a deliberate extra step

Apple Mail can export mailboxes as .mbox packages and import messages from supported mbox sources. That is an important preservation feature, not something to dismiss. It is a mailbox-level workflow: select the mailbox, export it, and keep the resulting package somewhere you can recover later. Official guidance ↗

An export is only useful if the mailbox contains what you intended to preserve. For your own check, choose one old conversation, one attachment, and one sent message, then confirm they are present in the exported archive. Keep a second copy on another drive. A local archive still depends on the health of the computer and disk holding it. Official guidance ↗

Why MailVault is our choice for local ownership

MailVault is available on macOS, Windows, and Linux, and treats the saved local copy as part of the daily email experience. Custody indicators show whether a message lives on the server, in your vault, or both. Portable .eml files and local search make it easier to inspect an individual message later. MailVault also offers AI writing and thread summaries using a supported on-device model, alongside the underlying original mail.

Three checks before you switch

  1. Export a small mailbox and check that its oldest messages and attachments are present.
  2. Compare mailbox-level export with opening a single saved .eml file.
  3. If AI writing matters, check the model and hardware requirements on the Mac you actually use.

For MailVault, offline access covers messages and attachments already saved. Sending and synchronization need a connection. Keep a second backup on another disk. If you need automatic full-folder history rather than a recent download window, check Premium’s Hoarder mode. Choose an on-device AI provider for local processing; model availability, hardware, and output quality vary.
