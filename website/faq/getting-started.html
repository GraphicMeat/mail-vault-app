Getting Started

What MailVault is, which email providers it works with, what it runs on, and how it keeps your mail private.

What is MailVault?

MailVault is a free, open-source desktop email client for macOS and Linux that stores your emails in a vault on your own disk. It connects to your email accounts via IMAP and saves all messages as standard .eml files (RFC 5322) in Maildir format on your computer, giving you a permanent offline backup with no vendor lock-in.

Which email providers does MailVault work with?

MailVault works with any email provider that supports IMAP, including Gmail, Outlook, Yahoo Mail, iCloud Mail, ProtonMail (with Bridge), Fastmail, Zoho Mail, and any self-hosted or business email provider with IMAP access.

What are the system requirements?

MailVault requires macOS 11 (Big Sur) or later, and runs natively on both Intel and Apple Silicon. On Linux it ships as .deb, AppImage, Snap and Flatpak for Ubuntu 22.04+ / Debian 12+. It stays in the menu bar and keeps syncing with the window closed.

Is my data secure?

MailVault is designed with privacy first. Your email credentials are stored in the macOS Keychain or the Linux Secret Service, never in a plain file. All data stays on your machine — there is no cloud sync, no tracking, no analytics, and no data mining. The app is open source so you can verify the code yourself.
