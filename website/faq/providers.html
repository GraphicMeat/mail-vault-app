Email Providers

Connecting Gmail, Microsoft 365, Outlook.com and Proton Mail, and sending from an alias.

Does MailVault work with Gmail and Outlook?

Yes. MailVault works with Gmail, Outlook, and any other email service that supports IMAP. For Gmail, sign in directly with your Google account using OAuth2 — no manual IMAP settings needed. Microsoft 365 Business and Enterprise accounts can sign in directly with your Microsoft account using OAuth2, and it works seamlessly with two-factor authentication. Personal Outlook.com accounts are currently affected by a known Microsoft server issue.

Why can't I connect my Outlook.com account?

There is a known Microsoft server-side issue affecting personal Outlook.com, Hotmail, and Live.com accounts since late 2024. After signing in with your Microsoft account, the IMAP connection fails with "User is authenticated but not connected." This affects all third-party email clients that use IMAP with OAuth2 — not just MailVault.

Microsoft requires OAuth2 for personal accounts. Unfortunately, their IMAP service has a regression that prevents OAuth2-authenticated sessions from connecting to the mailbox.

What you can do:

  • Microsoft 365 Business/Enterprise accounts are not affected — OAuth2 works normally for organizational accounts
  • Monitor the issue — This is a server-side problem that only Microsoft can fix. The Microsoft Q&A thread tracks the issue
  • Use the Outlook web app or Microsoft's official apps in the meantime for personal accounts
  • MailVault is ready — Once Microsoft resolves the issue, MailVault's OAuth2 sign-in will work immediately with no changes needed

For technical details: MailVault uses the same OAuth2 implementation as Mozilla Thunderbird, following Microsoft's official IMAP OAuth2 documentation with PKCE and XOAUTH2 authentication.

How do I use MailVault with Proton Mail?

Proton Mail encrypts your mail end-to-end, so it doesn't expose IMAP directly. Connecting it to MailVault (or any third-party mail client) requires Proton Mail Bridge — a desktop app from Proton that runs on your own computer and exposes your encrypted mailbox as standard IMAP/SMTP on 127.0.0.1. Bridge requires a paid Proton Mail plan; it isn't available on Proton's free tier.

Setup steps:

  1. Install Proton Mail Bridge from Proton's website
  2. Sign in to Bridge with your Proton account
  3. Bridge displays per-account IMAP/SMTP credentials — the password shown is a Bridge-generated app password, not your regular Proton account password
  4. In MailVault, choose Proton Mail Bridge in the provider list (or Custom IMAP if it isn't listed) and enter:
    • Server: 127.0.0.1
    • IMAP Port: 1143, Security: STARTTLS
    • SMTP Port: 1025
    • Username: your Proton email address
    • Password: the Bridge-generated password from step 3

Proton Mail Bridge must be running in the background whenever MailVault syncs — if Bridge is closed, MailVault can't reach the mailbox. Mail you've already archived stays fully readable and searchable in MailVault even while Bridge is closed, since MailVault is local-first and reads from its own .eml store rather than live from Bridge.

Bridge secures its local IMAP/SMTP server with a self-signed TLS certificate. MailVault handles this automatically for local (127.0.0.1) connections, so no manual certificate installation is required.

Can I send from a different address than the one I log in with?

Yes. Open Settings → Accounts, pick the account, and fill in Send Mail As. Leave it blank to send from your login address. This is common on Fastmail and on any provider where the address you sign in with is not the address you hand out.

Only the From address on outgoing mail changes. Your username and password stay exactly as they are, MailVault still signs in to IMAP and SMTP as the account you set up, and no Reply-To header is added — replies go to the address you send as.

Finding and checking the address:

  • Suggestions — MailVault offers addresses it has already seen in your own mail: ones you have sent as before, and ones that mail addressed to you arrives on.
  • Verify — sends a single test message from the address, to your own mailbox by default, so you can confirm your provider accepts it before you rely on it.
  • If it is refused — the address has to be an alias or identity your provider lets that login send from. Fastmail accepts any alias on the account; Gmail needs the address added under "Send mail as" in Gmail settings, and Microsoft 365 needs Send As permission from an administrator.

Aliases are configured with your email provider, not in MailVault — MailVault sends as an address your provider has already authorised.
