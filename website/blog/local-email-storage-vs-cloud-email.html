Local Email Storage vs Cloud Email: Which Copy Do You Control?

On this page
  • Separate delivery, synchronization and retention
  • Compare the practical trade-offs
  • Privacy depends on the whole arrangement
  • A setup that combines both
  • Choose based on what must survive
  • Related guides

Local email storage and cloud email solve different parts of the same problem. Your provider receives messages and makes the live mailbox available across devices. A local archive gives you a copy you can retain and inspect on your own computer. Many people need both.

Separate delivery, synchronization and retention

Delivery gets a message into your account. Synchronization reflects changes between a client and the server. Retention keeps a copy for later. Moving a message to a server folder named Archive changes its location in the mailbox; it does not automatically create a separate copy on your Mac.

In MailVault, explicitly archived messages live as .eml files in a local Maildir tree. The saved copy can remain after the message is removed from the provider. Before deleting server mail, confirm the archive completed and verify that the messages and attachments open. These file-layout instructions use the default Maildir format. Settings → Storage also offers JMAP storage, which keeps original message bytes with JSON records and deduplicates identical messages.

Compare the practical trade-offs

  • Access. Cloud mail is convenient across connected devices. A local archive is available on the computer holding it, including offline when full messages are saved.
  • Control. The provider manages the server copy. You choose where local archive files and backups live.
  • Recovery. A retained local copy can help if the account becomes unavailable. A separate backup protects against loss of the computer itself.
  • Storage. Server quotas and local disk space are separate limits. Downloading mail does not free server space; deleting verified server copies does.
  • Maintenance. A provider operates its service. You must keep local storage healthy, protect it and check your backups.

Privacy depends on the whole arrangement

Local search keeps archive retrieval on your computer. That does not make ordinary email end-to-end encrypted or stop the provider handling new mail. Local files also need protection from unauthorized access. If you place the vault in a cloud-synchronized folder, that folder’s service becomes another place where your archive is stored.

Cloud services differ in access controls, encryption and retention. Evaluate the actual service and settings instead of assuming all hosted storage behaves alike. For the Mac side of the decision, see our private archive checklist.

A setup that combines both

  1. Keep your existing provider for incoming and outgoing mail.
  2. Archive the folders and years you want to retain in MailVault.
  3. Test the saved messages and attachments without a connection.
  4. Back up the archive to a separate drive and check that copy too.
  5. Only then decide which server messages you no longer need online.

Choose based on what must survive

If your priority is convenient access from several devices, retain enough mail on the provider for that workflow. If old records must survive an account closure, keep a verified independent archive. If the Mac could fail tomorrow, add a tested backup rather than relying on the archive alone.
