Private Email Archive for Mac: Keep a Copy You Control

On this page
  • Follow the message, not the privacy slogan
  • Local storage is not the same as encryption
  • Keep search close to the archive
  • Make a privacy checklist for your actual setup
  • Who this approach suits
  • Related guides

A private email archive starts with knowing where the messages live, who can access them and where searches run. MailVault keeps archived mail as files on your Mac and searches it locally. You do not need a MailVault account or a MailVault-hosted mailbox to keep your copy.

Follow the message, not the privacy slogan

MailVault connects to your email provider to receive and send mail. Your provider still handles that account, and recipients still receive the messages you send. Local archiving changes where your retained copy lives; it does not remove the provider from ordinary email delivery.

The local vault stores individual .eml files in a Maildir directory. Those files contain message headers, bodies and attachments. That makes the archive portable and inspectable, but also means someone with access to the files may be able to read them.

The Mac App Store edition cannot move the working vault to another folder. A separate backup location can still be selected.

Local storage is not the same as encryption

Do not treat the vault as an encrypted container just because it is local. On a Mac, use a strong login password and review disk protection. Apple explains how FileVault protects data on your Mac. Protect external backup drives separately, and keep recovery information somewhere you can reach if the computer fails.

MailVault stores account credentials in the macOS Keychain. That protects credentials through the operating system; it is a different mechanism from protecting the archived message files. Likewise, hiding personal details on screen does not encrypt the underlying mail.

Keep search close to the archive

Local message search lets you retrieve saved correspondence without sending the query to a MailVault search service. It also works without a network connection when the required messages are saved. You can search by remembered text and narrow the results by sender, date or folder.

Make a privacy checklist for your actual setup

  • Check whether the vault or backup folder is inside a cloud-synchronized directory. If it is, that service becomes part of your storage arrangement.
  • Limit access to the macOS account and any shared or external drive holding mail.
  • Save full messages before going offline; a server-only message still needs the provider.
  • Keep a separate backup and test opening a copied message and attachment.
  • Review optional integrations before enabling them, especially if they send content to an external service.

Who this approach suits

A local archive suits someone who wants a readable copy of personal or business correspondence under their own control. It also makes the computer and backup drives their responsibility. A single laptop is not enough protection against theft, failure or accidental deletion.
