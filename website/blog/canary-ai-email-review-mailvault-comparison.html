A client that distinguishes local and cloud work

Canary’s AI documentation says triage, prioritization, and categorization run on the device, while drafts, summaries, and natural-language answers use hosted models. It states that email is not used to train generalized AI models. This is a useful distinction: a no-training commitment concerns data use, while on-device processing concerns where the work happens. Official guidance ↗

Someone deciding between AI clients should ask more than whether an app is described as private. Try three separate actions: ordinary search, a new summary, and a draft. Establish which require a connection and which involve remote processing. That makes the tradeoff concrete and lets you choose the setting that fits each kind of correspondence.

Local inbox intelligence does not make every AI feature offline

Canary documents offline triage and ordinary search for downloaded mail, plus cached summaries. New summaries, drafts, and natural-language answers need a connection. Its description of natural-language search says the query and a limited set of locally selected results are sent to generate an answer. That is a hybrid workflow, not entirely on-device generative AI. Official guidance ↗

The current pricing page lists Free for core inbox use, Growth at $36 per year for AI and productivity features, and Pro+ at $100 per year for additional security including PGP and SecureSend. Lifetime options also exist. Encryption and backup answer different questions: protecting a message does not by itself demonstrate that you can recover it after losing the original account. Why MailVault is our choice for local ownership

MailVault offers AI writing and thread summaries using a supported on-device model, including Apple’s model where available or a downloaded model. Compatible endpoints are another option, so configure a local provider if local processing is the goal. The underlying mail remains saved as portable .eml files, and ordinary full-text search uses a local index. That combination makes MailVault our choice for keeping both the originals and the AI workflow close to home.

Three checks before you switch

  1. Distinguish ordinary local search from a generated natural-language answer.
  2. Check which encryption tools are included in the selected license.
  3. Test a new summary using MailVault’s configured local model, not a remote endpoint.

For MailVault, offline access covers messages and attachments already saved. Sending and synchronization need a connection. Keep a second backup on another disk. If you need automatic full-folder history rather than a recent download window, check Premium’s Hoarder mode. Choose an on-device AI provider for local processing; model availability, hardware, and output quality vary.
