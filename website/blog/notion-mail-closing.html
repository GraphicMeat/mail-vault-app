Notion Mail Is Closing: What to Save, and What MailVault Can Preserve

6 min read

Notion Mail is shutting down on September 22, 2026. If you used it as your inbox, the important distinction is that your Gmail messages are not disappearing with the app — but some useful pieces lived only inside Notion Mail and need to be saved before the shutdown.

Notion’s official shutdown guide says September 21 is the last day to export or migrate anything you want to keep. That leaves a short window to separate what belongs to Gmail from what belonged to the Notion Mail experience.

What you need to save

Notion lists four categories of Mail-only data: drafts, scheduled emails, snippets, and auto-label instructions. Migrate drafts and scheduled emails to Gmail. Export snippets and auto-label instructions, including any attached files, so you can recreate those workflows elsewhere. Email reminders and the custom views that organized your inbox will not transfer automatically.

Your existing email history remains in Gmail because Notion Mail synchronized with it. Mail blocks already placed on Notion pages also remain, and Notion says Gmail-connected AI workflows will continue. The inbox application itself — web, desktop, and iOS — is the part that is going away.

Notion Mail solved an organization problem

The appeal was not just reading and sending messages. Notion Mail let you create views for different kinds of work, group and filter messages, apply properties, write auto-label instructions, and keep an inbox shaped around your own priorities. It made Gmail feel more like a workspace.

That is a real need. Email is both a communication channel and a record of decisions, receipts, contracts, and conversations. An inbox view can make that record easier to work with, but it is still a view over someone else’s server. When the client changes, the organization layer may be the first thing you lose.

Where MailVault overlaps

MailVault is built for a different part of the problem: keeping a durable copy of the mail itself. It connects to IMAP and OAuth accounts, including Gmail and Outlook, and archives every folder locally on your own disk.

That gives you several adjacent capabilities:

  • Multiple accounts and folders. Keep mail from more than one provider in one desktop app, including Inbox, Sent, Drafts, Archive, and custom folders.
  • Offline access. Once messages are saved, you can read and search your local copy when the network or provider is unavailable.
  • Searchable history. Find messages across the local vault instead of relying on a provider account or a single inbox view.
  • Portable files. MailVault stores messages as individual .eml files, an open format that other mail tools can read. Your archive is not locked to MailVault.
  • Scheduled backups. Premium scheduled backups keep the local copy current without relying on memory. Manual, unlimited backups remain free.
  • Storage you control. Put the vault on an external drive, NAS mount, or another folder when you want the archive to live somewhere specific.

These features do not recreate Notion’s AI labeling, Notion pages, mail blocks, or meeting scheduling. They preserve the underlying email and make it available through a local, searchable, portable archive. That distinction matters: MailVault is a backup and mail-reading tool, not a Notion workspace in disguise.

A practical transition plan

Before September 22, open Notion Mail and move every draft and scheduled message to Gmail. Export snippets, auto-label instructions, and snippet attachments. Capture any custom views or reminder lists you will need to rebuild manually. Then verify that the important messages are present in Gmail — and create a second copy outside the provider.

MailVault can make that second copy without changing your Gmail account. Connect the account, let it sync every folder, and leave the local archive on your computer or an external location you control. If you later change mail clients, providers, or workflows, the .eml files remain yours.
