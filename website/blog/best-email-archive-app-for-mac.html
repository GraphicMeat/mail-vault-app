Best Email Archive App for Mac: How to Choose

The best email archive app for your Mac is the one that lets you retrieve an old message when the original account is unavailable. Start with that test. A familiar inbox, a large storage allowance or an Archive button does not, by itself, prove that you own an independent copy.

This guide is written by the makers of MailVault. It explains the checks we would make before choosing an archive, and where our app fits. For a wider shortlist, see our comparison of email archivers for Mac.

Choose for the job you actually have

If you need a one-time handover, a mailbox export may be enough. If you regularly retrieve client decisions, invoices or years of correspondence, choose an archive you can search and read every day. If a team needs centrally enforced retention or legal holds, evaluate a dedicated organizational archive against those requirements.

Apple Mail can export mailboxes as MBOX packages. That is a useful starting point for a manual copy; you still need a plan for updating, storing and testing it. Apple documents the steps in its mailbox export guide.

Five checks before you commit

  • Independent retention. Check whether a saved message remains after it leaves the server. A synchronized cache and a retained archive serve different purposes.
  • Complete messages. Open old messages and their attachments offline. A subject line in a search index is not the whole email.
  • Portable storage. Find out whether you can read or export your mail without renewing a subscription.
  • Useful retrieval. Try a real search by sender, date and a remembered phrase, including Sent and custom folders.
  • A second copy. Check where backups live and how you recover them if the Mac is lost.

Where MailVault fits

MailVault combines a desktop email client with a local archive. Messages you archive are stored as standard .eml files in a Maildir tree, with their headers, bodies and attachments. You can read and search saved mail offline and export messages as MBOX when moving to another tool.

Connecting an account is only the beginning. Download modes control what is kept locally; a recent cache is not a complete historical archive. Deliberately archive the folders and years you need, then inspect the per-message state before removing anything from the server.

What is free, and when to pay

MailVault includes manual archiving, unlimited manual backups and local message search for free. Premium adds scheduled backups, attachment-content search and combined archive-and-delete operations. Choose automation because you need it, after proving that the archive fits your workflow.

A sensible first trial is one folder and one year of mail. Archive it, disconnect from the network, open several messages and attachments, and export a sample. Add a backup on a separate drive. If those checks pass, expand to the rest of your history.
