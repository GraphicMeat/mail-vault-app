Microsoft Just Doubled Your Mailbox to 100GB — And Raised the Price 8–17%

6 min read

Between July and September 2026, Microsoft is rolling out a change that sounds unambiguously good: Exchange Online mailboxes on Microsoft 365 Business Basic, Standard, and Premium are doubling from 50GB to 100GB per user, at no extra cost, no add-on to buy, and no separate archive mailbox to enable. New purchases and renewals started getting it July 1; the rollout to existing tenants is expected to finish by the end of September.

The catch, which Microsoft did not put in the same headline, is that this storage bump arrives bundled with a broader 2026 pricing update — and across the affected Microsoft 365 Business licenses, that update raises prices by roughly 8–17%, depending on plan. You're not getting more mailbox for free. You're getting more mailbox as the justification for paying more.

Why This Reads as Generous

Storage anxiety is one of the most common reasons people go looking for an email tool in the first place — "mailbox full" errors, the annual ritual of deleting old newsletters to make room for one more attachment, IT tickets asking for a quota increase. Doubling the ceiling from 50GB to 100GB genuinely fixes that specific pain for a lot of business users who were bumping into it. It's a real improvement, and it's reasonable that Microsoft is marketing it as one.

But storage anxiety and data risk are two different problems, and this change only touches the first one.

A Bigger Box Is Still One Box

Whether your mailbox holds 50GB or 100GB, the fundamental architecture hasn't changed: every message you've ever sent or received lives in one place, controlled by one company, governed by one set of terms of service you didn't write and can't negotiate. A larger quota means you can procrastinate on cleaning up for longer. It doesn't mean:

Your data survives a lockout. Lose access to the 2FA method tied to the account and 100GB of email is exactly as unreachable as 50GB was — the recovery flow doesn't get friendlier because the mailbox got bigger.

Your data survives a policy change. Microsoft can adjust storage tiers, feature gates, or dormant-account rules again next year, the same way it just did this year. A bigger quota today is not a promise about the quota, or the terms attached to it, two years from now.

Your data is recoverable if Microsoft's systems fail. Under Microsoft's own shared responsibility model, the platform commits to infrastructure uptime — it does not guarantee full recovery of your specific deleted or corrupted mail. That responsibility sits with you, the same as it did before the storage increase.

None of this is a knock on Exchange Online as a mail platform. It's a reminder that "more room in the provider's building" and "a copy of your data outside the provider's building" solve completely different risks, and only one of them just got bigger.

The Price Side Is the Part Worth Reading Twice

An 8–17% increase across Business Basic, Standard, and Premium licenses is not trivial at any real seat count. If you're evaluating whether the new 100GB ceiling is worth what you're now paying for it, the honest framing is: you're paying more for a bigger version of the same single point of failure. That's a fine trade if storage was genuinely your bottleneck. It's not a trade that buys you resilience, portability, or an exit path if the relationship with Microsoft ever changes on terms you don't like.

Own a Copy, Regardless of the Provider's Box Size

The fix for "my data only exists inside one company's infrastructure" isn't a bigger allotment inside that infrastructure — it's a second copy outside it. We wrote about what an actual email backup looks like in more depth previously; the short version is the classic 3-2-1 principle scaled down for a mailbox: the live server copy you already have, plus a local copy on a device you control that doesn't depend on anyone's login, quota, or terms of service.
