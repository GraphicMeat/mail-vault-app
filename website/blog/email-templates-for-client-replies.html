Email Templates for Client Replies: Three Reusable Examples

On this page
  • Three client reply templates you can adapt
  • Save the wording once in MailVault
  • Insert it where the reply needs it
  • A short check before every send
  • Keep the library small enough to use

By MailVault · 4 min read

A client asks what you need before you can quote. You know the answer, because you wrote almost the same email yesterday. You find it in Sent, copy the paragraph, change the name and hope you did not leave the previous project’s deadline behind.

An email template saves the repeatable part of a reply so you can spend your time on the details that changed. In MailVault, save a named template under Settings → Templates, insert it into a draft from the Templates menu, then edit it before sending. Templates are part of the free app.

The aim is to stop rebuilding useful wording from memory. Start with three replies you already send regularly, rather than a library of messages you might never use.

Three examples · Save a template · Insert and edit · Review before sending

Three client reply templates you can adapt

The bracketed text below is a reminder for you to replace manually. MailVault inserts the saved text; these examples do not use automatic name, project or date substitution.

1. Ask for the details needed to quote

Hi [name],

Thanks for getting in touch about [project]. To put together a useful quote, could you send the scope, any reference material, and your preferred deadline?

If there is a fixed budget or a particular deliverable you need, please include that too. Once I have those details, I can confirm the next step.

Save this as Quote — request details. Before sending, remove questions the client already answered. Repeating a question from the previous message makes a reusable reply feel careless.

2. Acknowledge files without promising an unchecked deadline

Hi [name],

Thanks, I have received [files or material]. I will review them against [agreed scope] and let you know if anything is missing.

I will send you an update by [date and time zone].

Save this as Files — received. Check that the attachments or shared links actually open, then choose a date you can meet. If you have not received everything, say exactly what is missing instead.

3. Follow up on one specific decision

Hi [name],

I am following up on [proposal or question] from [date]. Could you confirm [the decision needed]?

If the timing has changed, let me know and I can adjust the next step.

Save this as Follow-up — decision. Make the requested decision explicit: approve the proof, choose an option, or provide a missing file. A specific question is easier to answer than “Any updates?”

Save the wording once in MailVault

  1. Open Settings → Templates and choose Add Template.
  2. Enter a short name that describes when you would use it.
  3. Paste the reusable wording into the template body. Keep changing facts in obvious brackets.
  4. Choose Save. Use the edit control beside the template whenever the wording needs updating.

The saved template contains a name and body text. Plan to choose the recipient, subject and any attachments in the message itself. Avoid storing a specific customer’s private details in a template intended for everyone.

Insert it where the reply needs it

Open a new message or a reply, place the cursor where you want the text, and choose a template from Templates. MailVault inserts the body into the draft. You can change the inserted wording without rewriting the saved template.

For example, if the client has already supplied the deadline, remove that question from the quote template and ask only for the scope and references. Add a sentence that responds to their actual message. The template provides the starting point; your final reply should still fit the conversation.

A short check before every send

  • Replace every bracket. Look for leftover names, dates and project placeholders.
  • Check the account and recipients. Confirm the From address and who will receive a reply-all.
  • Check promises. A deadline or price copied from old wording still needs your approval.
  • Attach the file you mention. The template text does not attach it for you.
  • Read the whole draft. Remove repeated greetings, signatures and text you have already written above the insertion point.

Keep the library small enough to use

After a week, keep the templates you actually inserted and revise the lines you kept editing. Split a template only when two situations consistently need different wording. A small set of clear names is easier to choose from while a client is waiting.

Templates do not require AI to insert saved text. If you want help rewriting a particular draft, AI writing help is a separate feature. If the harder problem is remembering which messages still need attention, set up a daily reply review first.
