Best Email Backup Software for Mac: Choose by Recovery

On this page
  • Three approaches, three jobs
  • What to check in MailVault
  • Manual or scheduled backups?
  • Run a small recovery drill
  • Related guides

The best email backup software for your Mac should make recovery predictable. “Backup complete” is useful only if you know what was copied, where it went and how to open it again. Choose with a recovery test, especially before clearing a full mailbox or closing an account.

This guide is written by the makers of MailVault. Our app suits people who want a searchable email archive plus backups they control. A one-time export or a whole-Mac backup may also be part of the right setup.

Three approaches, three jobs

A mailbox export is useful for a one-time copy or transfer. Apple Mail exports selected mailboxes as MBOX packages; see Apple’s export instructions. Check which folders and messages are included and repeat the export when you need an updated copy.

A Mac backup protects files that are already on the computer. Apple’s Time Machine guide explains backup and recovery for the Mac. Do not assume a system backup contains emails that your mail app never fully downloaded. Check that your archive location is included.

An email archive with a separate backup keeps mail usable day to day and adds another copy for recovery. MailVault stores archived messages as .eml files and can back them up to a location you choose. The archive and its backup should not share the same single point of failure. These file-layout instructions use the default Maildir format. Settings → Storage also offers JMAP storage, which keeps original message bytes with JSON records and deduplicates identical messages.

The Mac App Store edition cannot move the working vault to another folder. A separate backup location can still be selected.

What to check in MailVault

Review the backup scope before running a job. Backing up only archived mail covers what is already saved in the vault. To include messages that are still only on the server, select the broader scope and keep the account connected while those messages are downloaded.

Choose a second location on a separate drive when protecting against disk failure. Another folder on the same disk does not protect against losing that disk. If the backup drive is normally disconnected, attach it before the job and check the result before putting it away.

Manual or scheduled backups?

Unlimited manual backups are free in MailVault. They suit a one-off archive or someone who already follows a regular backup routine. Scheduled backups are Premium and suit mailboxes that change daily. Scheduling saves repetition, but a job still needs access to its source and destination.

Run a small recovery drill

  1. Back up a folder containing an old message, a recent message and an attachment.
  2. Confirm the job finished and inspect the backup destination.
  3. Copy a sample message out of the backup and open it in a compatible mail app.
  4. Open its attachment and compare the sender, date and body with the original.
  5. Write down where the backup lives and how you would reach it from another Mac.

Do this before deleting any server messages. Premium combined archive-and-delete operations add checks to that workflow, but the broader recovery plan still matters. Start with a free manual backup, prove you can use the result, then decide whether automation is worth paying for.
