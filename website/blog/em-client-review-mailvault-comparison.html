A broad productivity workspace

eM Client offers desktop apps for Windows and macOS and mobile apps for Android and iOS. Its feature set includes calendars, tasks, notes, snoozing, translation, and message encryption, alongside email. It is a sensible candidate when those surrounding tools are part of your routine. Official guidance ↗

A freelancer might want to read a client message, check a date, and record the next task without changing apps. That is a useful workflow to test with a real appointment and a harmless sample message. The best fit is the one whose calendar and contact behavior matches your actual accounts, rather than the one with the largest collection of buttons.

Check the license against your actual use

eM Client’s free desktop license supports up to two accounts on one device and is for personal, non-commercial use. Paid Personal and Business options change the permitted usage and features, with subscription and one-time purchase routes. A free trial is not the same thing as an indefinitely free business setup. Official guidance ↗

eM Client’s support guidance also documents backup settings and restoration. It deserves credit for having a backup workflow. For an archive comparison, the useful question is what you would do after changing computers or no longer using that app: restore its database, export selected messages, or open individual email files directly. Try the recovery path before depending on it. Official guidance ↗

Why MailVault is our choice for local ownership

MailVault’s free app includes reading, composing, local search, and manual backups. Premium costs $4 per month or $25 per year and adds scheduled backups, snapshots, and other archive-management features. For someone keeping client records, the practical advantage is the ability to save mail as portable .eml files, inspect what is held locally, and make archive checks part of ordinary email use.

Three checks before you switch

  1. Count the accounts you need and confirm whether the license permits your work use.
  2. Run a small backup and restore it in a safe test setup.
  3. Compare the steps needed to recover one old attachment in each app.

For MailVault, offline access covers messages and attachments already saved. Sending and synchronization need a connection. Keep a second backup on another disk. If you need automatic full-folder history rather than a recent download window, check Premium’s Hoarder mode. Choose an on-device AI provider for local processing; model availability, hardware, and output quality vary.
