A good fit for a Microsoft-centered workday

Microsoft presents Outlook as a place for email, calendars, and contacts across desktop, mobile, and web. It supports personal, work, and school accounts, with connections to services such as Gmail and iCloud. Its appeal is the ability to handle correspondence and scheduling in a familiar Microsoft environment. Official guidance ↗

For an employee whose invitations, shared workflows, and support desk are built around Microsoft, compatibility can outweigh personal preferences. Start there: test a meeting invitation and your normal account permissions. If the organization supplies Outlook, you may already have the right daily tool. The remaining question is how you keep the messages you are permitted to retain when the account or job changes.

The Outlook version changes the export workflow

Microsoft’s current guidance includes PST export in new Outlook for Windows through Settings → Files → Export. The same guidance says importing a mailbox or calendar from PST into a mailbox still requires classic Outlook, and calendar/contact items in PST are unavailable in new Outlook. Check the exact version before building a recovery plan around it. Official guidance ↗

Microsoft also tells classic Outlook users to download all the mail they intend to export; the default download period may omit older messages. A successful export dialog is not sufficient proof that the archive contains your entire history. Open representative messages from several years and inspect their attachments before relying on the file. Official guidance ↗

Pricing and availability

Free Outlook options exist; classic desktop apps and additional features depend on the Microsoft 365 or Office license. Official guidance ↗

Why MailVault is our choice for local ownership

MailVault puts your saved messages on your own disk as .eml files and builds a local search index. You can use it for reading and replying as well as keeping a durable copy of important correspondence. For an independent archive, that gives you a straightforward path from a search result to the original message without designing your workflow around a PST container. For workplace accounts, verify the connection your organization permits.

Three checks before you switch

  1. Identify whether you use new Outlook, classic Outlook, or Outlook for Mac.
  2. Check that the oldest required mail is downloaded and included in your export.
  3. Open a saved message and attachment offline in each archive you are evaluating.

For MailVault, offline access covers messages and attachments already saved. Sending and synchronization need a connection. Keep a second backup on another disk. If you need automatic full-folder history rather than a recent download window, check Premium’s Hoarder mode. Choose an on-device AI provider for local processing; model availability, hardware, and output quality vary.
